# Magpie user guide

## What is Magpie

Magpie is a shared home for the things you find interesting, and a place to talk each one through with Maggie, the resident conversation partner. You collect curiosities (a question, a book, a strange fact, a half-formed idea) and the app helps you riff on each one: quick primers, gentle pushback, open questions, and a running chat. A magpie collects shiny things; your topics are the shiny things, and the app is the nest. The whole collection even renders as a living constellation, also called the Nest.

The one-liner: **Collect curiosities. Talk them through.**

## Quick start

Sixty seconds from curious to talking:

1. **Enter.** Go to magpie.wiki and tap "See the community nest" or "Add a curiosity" (if you arrive at the login screen instead, the one button there is "Enter Magpie"). One tap. No sign-up, no password. During the Gemini meetup there is also an "Enter the Gemini nest" button, which enters the separate event nest.
2. **Pick a topic.** "See the community nest" drops you onto the Nest constellation; the other buttons land on the Grid, a list of subjects like History or Music ("Add a curiosity" arrives with the add dialog already waiting). Tap a subject, then a topic that catches your eye. Or tap "Rediscover" in the bottom bar and let the app pick one at random.
3. **Riff.** The topic opens on the Maggie chat. If that topic's chat is still empty, Maggie greets you with a short question about it; if the community has already been talking there, you land in the saved thread and can just pick it up. Reply and go back and forth, or switch to the Thoughts tab and capture your own bullets. Talking a topic through for 3 to 5 minutes is the whole game. That is a riff.

Everything else in this guide is detail.

## Getting in

There is no sign-up anywhere in Magpie. Every way in is one click into a shared account.

**From the landing page.** "See the community nest" signs you in and drops you straight onto the Nest constellation. "Add a curiosity" signs you in and opens the Grid with the add dialog already waiting for your idea.

**From the login screen.** Open any in-app link while signed out and you are sent to the login screen: the wordmark, the line "Collect curiosities. Talk them through.", and one full-width button labeled "Enter Magpie". Tap it and you land on the Grid. The small print says it plainly: "One shared community space, for now. See what everyone is collecting, and add your own."

**What "shared" means.** Everyone who enters Magpie right now lands in the same community account. You all see the same subjects, topics, thoughts, and conversations. Anything you add, everyone can see, and vice versa. One nest, built together. (Private accounts come later; see the FAQ.)

If entry ever fails, the login screen shows "Could not open the community space. Tap to try again." Just tap "Enter Magpie" again. One quirk: if you got bounced to login from a specific page, entering always lands you on the Grid, not the page you were headed to.

One temporary note: during the Gemini meetup there is a separate event entry (the "Enter the Gemini nest" button and the magpie.wiki/gemini link). It signs you into a different shared nest, just for the meetup, and it goes away after the event. Content there does not appear in the community nest, or vice versa.

## The Grid

The Grid is home. It is a phone-width column, top to bottom:

- **A welcome hint**: "You're in the shared community, everyone's curiosities in one nest. Tap a subject to dive in, or add your own." Tap the X to dismiss it; it stays dismissed on that browser for a year.
- **The "Add a curiosity" button**: the front door for new ideas (covered below).
- **The search box**: placeholder "Search your wiki". Type and press Enter to see results.
- **The "Open your Nest" card**: "see your curiosity as a constellation". Same destination as the Nest tab.
- **Subjects**: "the buckets you care about". One row per subject, with its topic count and a chevron. Tap a row to see everything inside.
- **Recent Ideas**: the 6 newest topics, each with its subject underneath. The "Recent Ideas" heading is itself a link to the full recent list.

Inside a subject you get a back link to the Grid, an "Add a curiosity" button, and the topic list. If any topics carry facets, a row of filter chips sits above the list: an "All" chip first, then one chip per facet with its topic count, in alphabetical order. Tap a facet chip to filter to just those topics (the active chip turns teal), tap "All" to clear. The filter is per-visit and resets when you leave. The same chips appear above a collection's sub-topic list.

Two pieces of chrome frame every screen. The top bar holds the Magpie wordmark (always a tap back to the Grid) and a "Join the waitlist" button that hops out to the landing page. The bottom bar holds four tabs, in order: **Grid**, **Facets**, **Nest**, and **Rediscover**. The first three go places; Rediscover spins you to a random topic on the spot.

## Inside a topic

Open any topic and you will find the same anatomy, top to bottom:

- **A back link** to the subject it lives in (or, if it is part of a collection, back to that collection).
- **The meta row**: a subject dropdown pill, the topic's facet chips (each shown as #name), and a dashed "+ facet" input. The row is live: change the subject and it saves instantly, tap a chip's X to remove a facet, type a new one and press Enter. A tiny "saving..." note appears while changes land, and each chip links to that facet's cross-subject page.
- **The title**, big and serifed. (Titles are not editable, so enjoy what Maggie wrote.)
- **Five mode tabs**, in order: **Maggie**, **Brief**, **Challenge**, **Questions**, **Thoughts**. The active tab gets a teal underline. The Maggie tab (the chat) opens by default.
- At the very bottom, a faint little "delete topic" control. Tap it and it asks "delete this topic?" inline, with "yes, delete" and "cancel". Deletion is permanent and sends you back to the subject.

Each tab carries a small italic tagline: "talk it through" (Maggie), "the primer you needed earlier" (Brief), "the pushback you didn't see coming" (Challenge), "doors, not dead-ends" (Questions), "what's on your mind?" (Thoughts).

## The Maggie chat

The Maggie tab is the default, so it is the first thing you see on any topic, and it is a running conversation. On a topic whose chat is still empty, Maggie greets you with one short, lowercase question about that specific topic (it varies visit to visit; if the greeting fails to load you get her fallback: "what's your honest take on this one?"). The greeting is never saved, so it disappears once the thread has real messages. On a topic that already has a conversation, which is most of them on the shared account, you land in the saved shared thread instead and can just pick it up.

Type in the box (placeholder "Reply to Maggie..."), press Enter to send, Shift+Enter for a newline. Your message appears as a teal bubble, typing dots appear, and Maggie's reply streams in word by word: lowercase, brief, a smart friend at a party. The conversation is saved per topic, so come back next week and the whole thread is still there. On the shared account, everyone sees and continues the same conversation, which is part of the fun. One caution: leaving the chat while Maggie is mid-reply cancels the exchange and drops it from history, your message included. Let her finish.

## The AI modes

Beyond the chat, three of the tabs are single-shot AI text.

**Brief** gives you 3 to 5 numbered talking points: the primer you wish you had before the topic came up at dinner.

**Challenge** gives you one piece of pushback, led by a bold type tag (**Paradox**, **Hot take**, or **Steelman**) and a few sentences that poke at the obvious take.

**Questions** gives you 3 or 4 open questions. Doors, not dead-ends.

All three work the same way. The first time anyone opens a mode on a topic, a small spinner shows "asking maggie..." while it generates, then the text appears all at once. After that, the result is cached: every later visit loads near-instantly. On the shared account this cache is communal, so one person's generation is everyone's instant content. Want a different take? Tap **"Reroll"** at the bottom of the card. A fresh version replaces the old one for everyone (the previous version is gone for good). If something fails, an error shows with a "Try again" button; the old cached content survives failures.

## Capturing thoughts

The Thoughts tab (the last one) is where your own words live: bullet capture, fast and forgiving.

Above the input sits the **riff timer** (a pill showing 0:00 with a play icon) and your running count ("4 thoughts"). Tap the timer to start it counting up, tap again to pause, and use the little reset arrow to zero it. It is purely a pacer for your 3 to 5 minute riff: nothing happens when time passes, and it resets if you leave the page.

The input reads **"What's on your mind?"**. Type a thought and press Enter. That is the save. There is no Save button anywhere; the thought appears instantly and focus snaps back to the input so you can chain the next one. Before you have written anything, the empty list says "Catch thoughts as they come, edit later."

Editing is just as light. Tap any thought's text to edit it in place: Enter or tapping away saves, Escape cancels, and clearing all the text deletes it. Each thought also has an X on the right that deletes it immediately, no confirmation, no undo. Handle with care.

Once a topic has at least 3 thoughts, a purple **"Organize with AI"** button appears below the list. Tap it and Maggie sorts your raw bullets into up to four sections: **Insights**, **Counter-arguments**, **Follow-ups**, and **Topics to learn more about**. The result is ephemeral: not saved, gone when you switch tabs or reload. Tap the button again anytime for a fresh take.

## Adding a curiosity

The "Add a curiosity" dialog is the single way new topics enter the nest, and it asks almost nothing of you. Tap the button (on the Grid or any subject page) and a sheet slides up: heading "Add a curiosity", the italic line "catch it before it's gone", and the helper "tell maggie an idea. it files it under the right subject and tags it."

Type your idea in whatever rough shape it arrives. The placeholder shows the spirit of it: "e.g. high agency, why ravens hold funerals, the Fermi paradox". Press Enter (Shift+Enter for a newline) or tap "Add it". While Maggie works, the button reads "Maggie is filing..." and the dialog locks until she is done. On success you land directly on the new topic's page, ready to riff. If it fails, "hm, that one slipped away. try again." appears with your text still in the box. One quiet exception: if Maggie's AI is unreachable, the add still goes through with a fallback: the title is exactly what you typed, it lands in the "Ideas" subject, and no facets are set. It looks like a misfile but nothing is lost; refile it from Recent Ideas.

What Maggie does with your idea:

- **Writes the title.** She rephrases your idea into a specific conversation prompt, so the title may not match your exact words. Nothing is lost, though: your typed text is automatically saved as the topic's first Thought.
- **Picks the subject.** When you add from the Grid, she strongly prefers existing subjects and only invents a new one when nothing fits. If you opened the dialog from a subject page, that subject is locked in: Maggie still writes the title and tags but will not refile it elsewhere (though the umbrella check can still fold it into a collection).
- **Tags 1 to 3 facets.** Lowercase tags, reusing existing ones where possible.
- **Runs the umbrella check.** If your idea is about the same named entity (a book series, a team, a show, a franchise, a person) as existing topics, the related topics may get nested together under one collection. The check is deliberately cautious, so it only fires when Maggie is sure it is the same thing. A shared theme or genre never triggers this; that is what facets are for.

If you second-guess the filing, the safety net is **Recent Ideas**. Tap the "Recent Ideas" heading on the Grid to open the full list: up to the 100 newest topics, each with an inline Subject dropdown and facet editor. Change the subject, add or remove facets, and everything saves instantly with a small "saving..." indicator. The same inline editor sits at the top of every topic page.

## Collections

Sometimes several curiosities belong to the same thing: every book in a series, every angle on a team. Magpie folds those into a **collection** (a topic group) automatically via the umbrella check.

In a subject list, a collection looks like a normal topic row plus two tells: a small teal layers icon next to the title, and an italic line like "4 sub-topics inside". The sub-topics hide behind that row, keeping the subject list tidy.

Tap the row and you get the collection page: the title, a line reading "a collection · 4 sub-topics", and the list of its children, laid out just like a subject page. Two things differ from a normal topic. First, there are no mode tabs: you cannot capture thoughts or chat on the collection itself, only on its children. Second, the add button reads **"Add a sub-topic"**, and its dialog carries a tweaked helper line ("tell maggie an idea. it adds it to this collection and tags it."). Whatever you add goes straight into the collection, no second-guessing: an explicit add into a group skips the umbrella check because your placement wins.

Children of a collection back-link to the collection instead of the subject. At the bottom of a collection page the delete control reads "delete collection", confirming with "delete this collection and every sub-topic in it?". It does exactly what it says, permanently, so read it twice.

One honest limitation: you cannot create a collection by hand or move existing topics in or out of one. Collections form only when the umbrella check spots a match, or when you add sub-topics from inside one. A related caution: changing a collection's subject from its header moves only the collection, not its sub-topics. The children stay in the old subject and surface there as ordinary rows, though the collection page still lists them all.

## Facets

Subjects are where topics live. **Facets** are how they connect. A facet is a cross-cutting tag (paradox, fun facts, future) that ignores subject boundaries: a history topic and a music topic can share one, and browsing by facet shows them side by side.

Tap the **Facets** tab in the bottom bar. The index says "Facets" with the subline "where the connections live", then a wall of chips, one per facet, shown as #name with its topic count, ordered most-used first. The index doubles as a map of what the nest cares about.

Tap any chip to open that facet's page: every topic carrying it, drawn from all subjects, each row showing the topic's subject so the crossings are visible. You can also arrive here from any topic page by tapping one of its #facet chips.

## The Nest

The Nest is the whole wiki as a living constellation: every subject, topic, and facet a glowing node, every relationship a thread. Tap the **Nest** tab and it opens straight into the full-screen view, phones included.

**What you see.** Subject nodes are the largest circles, on the outer ring. Topics are the mid-size dots, sub-topics the smallest. Facets appear as diamond outlines that topics thread through, and faint purple threads (resonance) connect topics sharing two or more facets. The whole thing drifts gently, alive by default.

**Gestures.** Drag empty space to pan. Pinch or scroll to zoom. Drag a node to tug the physics around (it floats back when you let go; a toy, not a layout editor). Tap a topic to select it: its neighborhood lights up and a detail card appears. Tap a subject or facet node to light up its whole constellation. Double-tap any node to zoom in and center on it. Tap empty space to clear everything. The hint pill sums it up: "drag to pan · pinch or scroll to zoom · tap a node · double-tap to focus".

**The node card.** Tapping a topic shows a card with a kicker line (like "Topic · History"), the title, and its facet chips. Tap "Open topic →" to jump to that topic's page, or the × (or empty canvas) to dismiss.

**The controls.** The "Nest controls" panel docks top left. On phones it starts collapsed to just its header with "show"; tap to expand. Inside:

- **Facet dimension**: Bridge (facets as visible diamond hubs), Threads (hubs replaced by loops of co-tagged topics, cleaner when dense), or Off.
- **Toggles**: Resonance threads, Labels, and Living drift. Turn drift off to let the constellation settle and freeze.
- **Physics sliders**: Repel and Link length. Nudge them and the layout re-jiggles a moment later.
- **Subjects legend**: tap a subject to light up everything connected to it.
- **Light up a facet (across subjects)**: tap a facet chip to highlight every topic carrying it, in every subject. The fastest way to see the crossings.
- **Reset view**: clears highlights and rebuilds the whole constellation.

Switching Bridge, Threads, and Resonance keeps every node in place, so you can flip between dimensions without losing your mental map.

**Exiting.** Tap "Exit desktop view" top right to drop to the compact page (the graph in a card, a lighter set of controls, and a "Desktop" button to go back in). On a keyboard, Esc steps back progressively: card, then highlight, then exit. While the full view is open, the bottom tabs float as a rounded pill, so Grid, Facets, and Rediscover stay one tap away. Nothing you fiddle with is saved: next visit, the Nest opens fresh with defaults.

## Rediscover, search, and recent

Three ways the nest hands things back to you:

**Rediscover** is the fourth bottom tab. It is a button, not a page: one tap and you are on a random topic from the whole wiki. No animation, no ceremony, instant. It has no memory, so it can land on the same topic twice in a row. Perfect for "I want to riff but I don't know on what."

**Search** lives in the box on the Grid. It matches topic titles and facet names (case-insensitive, partial matches fine) and shows results newest first, with a count line like: 3 results for "wolves". It does not search your captured thoughts or chat history, just titles and tags.

**Recent Ideas** is the triage desk: the 100 newest topics, newest first, each editable in place (subject and facets). It is ordered by when topics were created, not when you viewed them, and it is the best place to give new additions a quick tidy.

## Talking instead of typing

Magpie is built for talking, so the two inputs where you do the most talking have a mic button beside them: the thought capture input on the Thoughts tab, and the reply box in the Maggie chat.

Tap the mic to start. It pulses teal while recording, and your words stream live into the input as you speak (appended after anything you already typed). Pauses do not stop it; it keeps listening until you tap the mic again.

The two spots behave differently on purpose:

- **On the Thoughts tab, stopping the mic is the save.** Whatever is in the field commits as a thought instantly. Speak, tap, done.
- **In the chat, stopping leaves the text in the box.** You get to fix any transcription hiccups before tapping send.

Voice works in Chrome and Edge (and Chrome on Android). In unsupported browsers like Firefox, the mic shows grayed out with the tooltip "Voice input needs Chrome or Edge". Voice recognition currently listens in English only; there is no language setting yet. Voice also needs an internet connection, since the browser's speech recognition runs in the cloud.

**On iPhone and iPad** the in-app mic is hidden entirely, because every iOS browser's speech engine silently returns nothing. Instead you will see a small hint under the input: "voice on mobile coming soon. type for now." The workaround: the mic key on the iOS keyboard should dictate into any Magpie input.

If the mic ever seems to fail silently, check under the input for a small dim message; it names the reason (permission blocked, no microphone, offline, or "didn't catch anything. give it another go.").

## Sharing the nest

The community nest is one account, one grid, one constellation, built by everyone who walks in. That is the point, and it works best with a little etiquette:

- **Everything you add is visible to everyone.** Topics, thoughts, chat messages. Add things you are happy to have neighbors read.
- **Everything is editable by everyone.** Someone may refile your topic or adjust its facets, and you can do the same. Assume good faith; usually it is someone tidying.
- **Rerolls are shared.** Rerolling a Brief, Challenge, or Questions replaces the cached version for everyone, and the old one is gone. Reroll when the current one is stale, not just to see what happens.
- **Chats are shared threads.** The Maggie conversation on a topic is one continuous thread anyone can pick up. Reading someone else's exchange and adding to it is fair play.
- **Deletes are forever.** There is no undo on deleting a thought, a topic, or a collection (which takes all its sub-topics with it). When in doubt, leave it in the nest.

In short: add generously, edit kindly, delete rarely.

## FAQ

**Do I need an account or password?**
No. No sign-up, no password, nothing to forget. Every way in is one click into the shared community space.

**Can I get my own private nest?**
Not yet. Everyone shares the community account for now, and the waitlist is the path to a personal one: tap "Join the waitlist" in the app's top bar (it takes you to the "Get on the list." section on the landing page), drop your email, and you will get exactly one note when it is ready. No spam, no marketing emails, and no confirmation email either; the button changing to "thanks, you're in" is the whole handshake.

**Can I export my thoughts or conversations?**
Not yet. There is no download or backup feature today.

**Is there a settings screen?**
Not yet, which also means Maggie's name cannot be changed yet (the design supports renaming her, the screen just is not built).

**Can I sign out?**
There is no sign-out button. With no personal account to protect, there is nothing to sign out of.

**Can I rename a topic?**
No. Titles are Maggie's department. You can change a topic's subject and facets anytime from its header or from Recent Ideas, and your original wording survives as the topic's first Thought.

**Can I undo a delete?**
No. Deleting a thought, topic, or collection is immediate and permanent.

**What are Glints and Draw Out?**
Future features, marked "Coming soon" on the landing page. Neither is usable yet; Rediscover is the resurfacing that exists today.

**Will Magpie ping me?**
No. No notifications, streaks, feeds, or engagement emails. The landing page makes the promise plainly: pull, never push, and no ads, ever.

**What is the Gemini nest?**
A temporary, separate shared account for the Gemini meetup, reachable during the event via magpie.wiki/gemini. It is not the community nest, and it goes away after the event.
