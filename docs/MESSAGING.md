# Magpie · Messaging

The full tagline hierarchy. One line per surface. Never stacked.

This document is locked. Do not invent new taglines. If a surface needs copy, pick the right line from this list.

For feature-level taglines (Subjects, Topics, Glints, Draw Out, etc.), see `MICROCOPY.md`.

---

## The hierarchy

### Hero / Brand line (the soul, now leading)
**Collect curiosities. Talk them through.**

Where it lives: magpie.wiki hero, logo lockup, in-app, footer, app icon splash. Anywhere the brand introduces itself.

Why it leads: the product is distinctive enough that a clarity headline does not actually clarify. Lean into mechanism plus invitation. The line is unmistakably Magpie. Fraunces italic accent on the period treatment.

### Hero sub (under the brand line)
*A personal wiki for the things you find interesting, with a conversation partner who remembers.*

Where it lives: directly under the brand line on the landing hero. Does the explaining work so the hero can stay poetic.

Why it works: names both halves of the product (wiki + conversation partner). Honest about what makes Magpie different from a chatbot ("who remembers").

Note (2026-06-10): the hero sub said "shiny" through launch prep. Swapped to "interesting" for cold-traffic comprehension; "shiny" now lives where the metaphor explains itself (the Glints definition line and the "Pull, never push" card on the landing). Root and og metadata mirror the hero sub.

### Second beat (the memorable line, screenshot-worthy)
**What you collect comes out in conversation.**

Where it lives: second scroll panel of the landing page, pitch deck second slide, the line that connects curiosity to charisma in one breath.

Why this position: by the time someone reads it, they have already chosen to keep reading. Reward them with the actual thesis: the curiosity you collect does not sit on a shelf, it becomes conversation. Names both halves of Magpie (wiki + chat) without naming a feature. Reads as a quiet claim, not a slogan.

### Manifesto banner (the philosophical claim)
**Curiosity is charisma, slowed down.**

Where it lives: top of the manifesto or about page, pitch deck cover slide, the line that does the heavy lifting in long-form writing or talks.

Why it earns this slot: it makes a real claim about how charisma actually works. The kind of line people quote-tweet. Risky if used in the wrong place because it sounds like a thinkpiece headline, but in the right slot (a slow page, a deck cover) it becomes the line the brand is remembered for.

### Draw Out feature line (the room line, where it belongs)
**Be the most interesting person in the room by making everyone else feel like one.**

Where it lives: the Draw Out feature page header, the Draw Out card on the homepage, Draw Out pitch material. Anywhere Draw Out is the specific subject.

Why this slot, not the second-beat slot: this line is the Draw Out promise in one sentence. It was the original second-beat candidate but it is too feature-specific to carry the whole brand thesis. It absolutely belongs on Draw Out's home page, where it is exactly the right size for the job.

### Secondary brand line (the bio cousin)
**Bring out the best in every conversation.**

Where it lives: about page, social bios (X, LinkedIn, Instagram), App Store description first line. Anywhere there is less room than the manifesto banner allows.

Why it works: tighter cousin of the room line, carries the draw-out angle in five words. Works as a default bio when nothing more specific fits.

### Demoted: the old clarity hero
**Get better at the conversations that matter.**

Where it lives: email subject lines, LinkedIn captions when announcing the product, App Store subtitle, A/B test candidate if the brand-led hero underperforms on cold traffic.

Why demoted from hero: it does honest clarity work but it sounds like every other conversation-skills product. Magpie is too distinctive for a generic clarity line as the front door. The line is kept available because clarity for cold-traffic email is a real job, and this line does that job well.

## The rule: one line per surface

Never stack two taglines on the same screen. The fastest way to make great copy feel cheap is to crowd it. If a surface feels like it needs more, what it needs is more whitespace, not more words.

Rotation across pages and posts is how you get mileage from the whole set without diluting any single line.

## What NOT to use

Resist these even though they sound fine:
- Anything with "AI-powered"
- Anything with "unlock," "amazing," "discover insights," "next level"
- Anything with the word "coach" (too small, too corrected-feeling for what Magpie actually is)
- Anything that leads with a feature ("Practice conversations with AI")
- Any line that uses em dashes (use periods, commas, parentheses, or colons)

## A/B test plan for launch

The brand-led hero ("Collect curiosities. Talk them through.") is the locked incumbent. If conversion on cold traffic underperforms, the candidate to swap in is "Get better at the conversations that matter." with the brand line moving to the second-screen position. Everything below the fold stays the same regardless of which hero wins.

For the second beat, "What you collect comes out in conversation." is the incumbent. The challenger is the original room line. If A/B testing tools become available post-launch, that is the test to run.

## Tone reminders

- Voice is editorial, not marketing
- Concrete over abstract: "five minutes" beats "a few minutes"
- Second person: "your topics," "your thoughts"
- Lowercase Maggie in chat, never in UI copy
